# How people make game pixel art with AI (as of Oct 2026)

Research notes for Rexi's Revenge. Every claim has a source next to it. **[UNVERIFIED]** means the claim comes only from a vendor, an SEO comparison page, or my own estimate.

**Short answer:** serious indie pipelines in 2025–2026 do one of two things:

- **(a)** use a pixel-native model (PixelLab, Retro Diffusion), which outputs true grid pixels, offers palette control, and has animation and rotation endpoints built in; or
- **(b)** generate with a general model (Nano Banana, GPT Image), then **reconstruct the grid** (unfake / pixel-art-fixer / Pixel Snapper / proper-pixel-art), lock a palette, and **hand-fix in Aseprite**.

Nobody credible reports usable many-frame action animation from video models without heavy cleanup. Code-driven parts animation and 3D-to-pixel pipelines are the established way to get many frames cheaply.

**Takeaway:** PixelLab is the only tool that treats a sprite as a rig you can pose. That makes it the best match for "many frames of _our_ hero". RD is the best for palette-locked one-shot assets and tilesets. **Neither is available through OpenRouter**, so each needs its own account and credits.

## 2. General image models for pixel art

- **They don't produce true pixels.** Diffusion and autoregressive image models work in latent space and have no concept of a discrete pixel. You get fake pixels on a drifting, fractional grid (for example 19.6 px instead of 20), every "pixel" a slightly different shade, with anti-aliased edges. ([jenissimo dev.to, Jul 2025](https://dev.to/jenissimo/how-to-tame-your-ai-pixel-art-3pk5), [asepix](https://github.com/flores666/asepix))
- **Nano Banana Pro lessons** from a developer write-up (Mar 2026, [roboticape](https://roboticape.com/2026/03/07/generating-game-sprites-with-gemini-image-generation-nano-banana-pro-lessons-learned/)):
  - There is **no real alpha**. A dark background plus threshold keying gives halos.
  - What works: a **pure #00FF00 background**, a **2–3 px white outline** around each sprite as an anti-alias buffer, and **HSV keying** (hue ±22°, S≥0.3, V≥0.3).
  - Prompt style: "CRITICAL" headers, hex colours, explicit bans ("NO gradients, NO noise").
  - Keep sheets to **3–6 items per row and one character per sheet**. The grid is not uniform, so use **content-aware slicing** (find empty columns), not uniform division.
- **Consistency levers:**
  - Reference images: Nano Banana 2/Pro accept up to 14, GPT Image 2.x up to 16, Seedream up to 14 (OpenRouter catalog via `list_models`).
  - Edit mode against a master frame (our sibling-project approach).
  - Seed locking where supported (Seedream, Qwen, FLUX.2). Seed tips for Nano Banana come from SEO pages **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Which general model is most grid-faithful?** I found **no rigorous public benchmark** for pixel-grid fidelity. 2026 head-to-heads only cover general quality: GPT Image 2 is better at spatial logic and pixel-stable over sequential edits, Nano Banana 2 at lighting and textures ([ropewalk](https://ropewalk.ai/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-2-vs-imagen-4-vs-flux-2-2026), [openart](https://openart.ai/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-2/)). Recraft offers a `pixel_art` substyle ([Recraft styles](https://www.recraft.ai/docs/api-reference/styles)), but its output is still fake pixels **[UNVERIFIED for v4]**. **Treat this as a question for our own A/B test.**
- **Generate big, then reconstruct.** A newer fix: unfake.js v1.4 handles **fractional and elastic grids** because newer models draw them ([release](https://github.com/jenissimo/unfake.js/releases/tag/v1.4.0)).

## 5. Recommendation for Rexi's Revenge (~$6)

**The core idea:** generate a few high-quality _static_ pieces, make them true pixels, and get the _many frames_ from code and rigs, not from more generations. Generation is cheap; consistency is what costs.

### Pipeline

1. **Hero master + parts (OpenRouter, Nano Banana Pro or 2).**
   - One side-view master at 2K on #00FF00 with a white outline buffer ([roboticape](https://roboticape.com/2026/03/07/generating-game-sprites-with-gemini-image-generation-nano-banana-pro-lessons-learned/)), then one edit call producing a **parts sheet**: torso+head, upper/lower arm, hand, thigh/shin/foot, gun arm.
   - Reconstruct with **pixel-art-fixer (Rust)** or **Pixel Snapper (WASM)**, snapped to our #24 palette.
   - Hand-fix the outline and face in Aseprite.
2. **Body animation (run/jump/hurt/idle):** a **code cut-out rig with IK** over the parts, as we already have in the sibling project. Bake to sprite frames offline so each limb segment is rotated with RotSprite/CleanEdge-style rotation, not live nearest-neighbour. Add 2–3 hand-drawn or AI-edited "smear/squash" keyframes only where the rig looks stiff (jump apex, hurt).
3. **16-direction aim:** draw or generate **the arm+gun as a separate part**. Because left and right mirror, you need **9 unique angles** (up, down, and 7 between). Pre-bake them with RotSprite and touch up the 2–3 worst. Do not ask an image model for 16 consistent angles.
4. **Flying enemies:** these suit a single static sprite with **code animation** (bob, tilt, blinking lights, rotor or propeller frames as 2–3 palette-cycled or swapped parts). One generation each, then reconstruct.
5. **Arena backdrop:** one wide generation (16:9), downscaled through the grid tool to 480×270 or 640×360, quantized to the palette, and split into parallax layers by hand or by edit calls. Fake pixels hurt backdrops less than characters.
6. **Optional, outside the $6:** PixelLab's free trial ([no card](https://www.pixellab.ai/pricing)) for a 128 px canvas skeleton-animated run cycle, to compare against the rig. At ~$0.05 per skeleton call ([API](https://www.pixellab.ai/pixellab-api)) it is the best paid upgrade if the rig looks stiff.

### Try first

A **$0.5 A/B test**: the same hero prompt on Nano Banana Pro, Nano Banana 2 and GPT Image 2 (with a reference image), each run through pixel-art-fixer and Pixel Snapper. Pick the model whose _reconstructed_ output needs the fewest hand fixes. No public benchmark answers this.

### Avoid

- Video→frames for action moves (they boil and blur).
- Asking a model for full multi-frame sheets of the _finished_ hero (detail drift).
- Per-frame custom palettes.
- Runtime rotation of pixel sprites.
- Expecting real alpha from Gemini.
- Subscription tools (God Mode at $29/mo, Ludo at $20/mo) on this budget.
